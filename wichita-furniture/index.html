Wichita Furniture + The Gallery

Choosing the right Shopify structure for two brands and what's coming next.

Wichita Furniture is the established business — a multi-store retail furniture operation with significant in-store revenue. The Gallery is the new luxury concept — first physical store opened January 2026, online presence to be built. With at least two storefronts planned and additional sub-brands on the roadmap, this document walks through three viable Shopify contract structures and the tradeoffs of each.

Setup

Two brands. One platform decision.

Each brand has a distinct profile, but they share infrastructure decisions — platform, integrations, partner engagement. The right Shopify structure depends on how the two brands relate to each other operationally and financially.

Wichita Furniture

Established · multi-store retail · STORIS today
  • Established furniture retailer with significant in-store revenue
  • Currently running on STORIS for retail operations and e-commerce
  • Migration to Shopify in progress, with Endertech as implementation partner (STORIS ↔ Shopify integration)
  • Goal: a more flexible e-commerce platform that scales with brand expansion

The Gallery

New luxury concept · pre-revenue online · brand-new physical store
  • First Gallery physical store opened January 2026, located at the Wichita Furniture site
  • Online presence to be built — clean slate for the brand's digital experience
  • Additional sub-brands on the roadmap beyond Gallery
  • Brand identity goal: a luxury, design-led experience distinct from Wichita Furniture's mass-market positioning
The key fork: whether Wichita Furniture and The Gallery operate as the same business entity (shared payroll, accounting, tax, bank accounts) or as fully separate businesses. That answer drives which of the three structures below is the right fit.
Decision factors

Five inputs that shape the right structure.

Each structure below fits a different combination of these factors. Aligning on these first ensures the contract shape matches how the business actually operates today and where it's heading.

01Business entity structure
Are Wichita Furniture and The Gallery separate business entities (different payroll, accounting, tax, bank accounts)? Separate entities point toward separate Shopify stores. Same business under one umbrella opens up a unified single-domain structure that's often operationally cleaner.
02Future brand expansion
How many sub-brands are on the roadmap beyond Wichita Furniture and The Gallery? More brands strengthens the case for a unified instance that can scale brand sections without standing up new Shopify stores each time.
03Brand differentiation depth
How visually distinct should each brand feel? Shopify themes are one-per-store, but heavily differentiated look-and-feel per brand within a single store is achievable through Liquid templates, custom sections, and brand-specific landing pages.
04Implementation approach
Partner-led (Endertech) or in-house build? The unified single-domain option benefits especially from partner expertise on SEO architecture, multi-brand Liquid setup, and STORIS connector design.
05Migration scope
What's moving over from STORIS — product catalog, customer records, historical orders, content, redirects? Migration scope shapes implementation timeline and partner engagement.
Why not expansion store

Expansion store isn't a fit for Wichita Furniture + The Gallery.

Shopify expansion stores are reserved for specific use cases — same-brand geographic extensions (e.g., a US store and a Canadian store) or B2B versions of the same brand. There are five qualifying criteria below, and all five must be met. The Wichita Furniture / Gallery pairing doesn't meet any of them, which is why expansion store isn't one of the three structural options.

D2C Expansion Store Internal Checklist — all 5 criteria must be met
✓ Brand name must be identical in URLs and landing pages
✓ Logo must be identical to main brand
✓ Products must be identical to main brand
✓ Store theme must be identical to main brand
✓ Store must be live (in production)
Why this pairing doesn't qualify: The Gallery is being built as a distinct brand — its own name, its own logo, its own curated luxury catalog, and its own visual identity. That's the opposite of "identical to Wichita Furniture" across every criterion. The Gallery is also pre-revenue / pre-launch online, so the "live in production" requirement isn't met either. The three structures below (two Plus stores · one Plus with multi-brand sections · Plus + Advanced) are the viable options.

When this option fits
  • Full Plus feature set on both brands: Flow, Functions, B2B channel, checkout extensibility, audiences, Launchpad, higher API limits — available to The Gallery from day one.
  • Plus card rates on both brands (2.25% + 30¢ standard, 2.95% + 30¢ Amex/commercial): ~25bps lower than Gallery would pay on Advanced — material savings as the brand scales.
  • Account-managed support on both: dedicated success team for each contract.

Tradeoffs to consider
  • $27,600/year of additional license cost vs. Option 2 for a pre-revenue brand. Paying $2,300/mo to host The Gallery from day one is harder to justify before revenue exists.

  • No shared customer view: a Wichita customer who buys at The Gallery shows up as a stranger; unified marketing, loyalty, and retention require an external CDP layer.
  • Adding "a couple more" brands compounds the problem: each new brand requires another Plus contract.
Option 2 · Recommended

One Shopify Plus store. One domain. Multi-brand within a single instance.

One Shopify Plus instance hosts Wichita Furniture, The Gallery, and future sub-brands as distinct brand sections under a unified domain. Different brand look and feel, shared backend — one cart, one checkout, one STORIS connector. Each brand operates as a section of the same store, with its own pages, navigation, and visual identity. This is the recommended path — lowest TCO, lowest operational complexity, full Plus feature set across every brand, and the cleanest fit for a multi-brand roadmap.

Why this is the recommended option
  • Lowest total cost of ownership: one Plus license, one set of card rates, one connector, one app stack — meaningfully cheaper across every dimension than Options 1 or 3.
  • One STORIS connector to build and maintain: Endertech builds the integration once, the internal team maintains one integration layer — significant time and cost savings over the 3-year horizon.
  • One product database, one customer record, one cart: unified inventory, unified loyalty and retention, and customers can shop across brands in a single transaction.
  • Full Plus feature set across all brand sections: Flow, Functions, checkout extensibility, audiences, B2B, Launchpad — available to every brand within the instance, including new sub-brands when added.
  • Adding new sub-brands is light-lift: spin up a new brand section within the existing instance instead of standing up a whole new Shopify store + connector each time. Aligns directly with the multi-brand roadmap.
  • Operational fit when entities are unified: if Wichita and Gallery share payroll, accounting, tax, and payouts, the contract structure mirrors how the business actually operates.
  • Brand differentiation is still possible: distinct look and feel per brand section via Liquid templates, sections, and custom landing pages. The Gallery can present as luxury; Wichita as mass-market — same store, different presentation layers.
Tradeoffs to consider
  • One Shopify theme per store: two literally distinct Shopify themes aren't possible — though look and feel per brand can be heavily differentiated via custom Liquid templates and sections.
  • One ad account per integration: single Facebook, single Instagram, single Google Ads connection per Shopify store. If brands need separate ad accounts, this is a real constraint.
  • SEO migration requires careful architecture: moving Wichita Furniture content under a unified domain structure requires careful URL architecture and 301 redirects. Best supported by an experienced implementation partner.
  • Brand distinction risk: if The Gallery is intended to feel fully independent of Wichita Furniture, sharing a domain may dilute that positioning.
  • Breaks down if entities are separate: different payroll, tax, payouts, and bank accounts make co-hosting on one Shopify instance impractical — in that case, fall back to Option 3.
  • Future divestiture is more complex: if Gallery is ever spun off or sold, untangling it from a unified instance is harder than separating two standalone stores.

Why Plus for Wichita Furniture

Plus is our enterprise plan for established, large retailers.

Shopify Plus is purpose-built for established multi-channel retailers operating at scale — businesses with significant revenue, ERP integrations, multi-brand operations, and the need for custom commerce logic and marketing automation. Shopify Advanced sits one tier below, positioned for small single-brand DTC merchants — typically under ~$2M GMV, with one storefront, no B2B, and no custom commerce logic. Wichita Furniture's profile puts it firmly in the Plus tier: established multi-channel retailer, STORIS ERP integration, multi-brand roadmap, and marketing-led operations.

What Advanced is built for

The small-shop ceiling
  • Single-brand DTC merchants, typically <$2M GMV
  • One storefront — no multi-brand support
  • Standard checkout — no customization at checkout
  • No Shopify Flow (manual ops + basic apps only)
  • No Shopify Functions (no custom commerce logic)
  • No B2B channel
  • API rate limits sized for a single-store operation
  • Self-serve / pooled support — no dedicated success team
  • Higher card rates (~2.5% + 30¢ online vs. Plus's ~2.25%)

What Wichita Furniture needs

Plus-tier requirements
  • Higher API rate limits — STORIS sync requires throughput Advanced can't deliver reliably at scale. This is the single most important reason Plus is the right plan for Wichita.
  • Lower card processing rates — material savings on every transaction once online scales (cost math below)
  • Checkout extensibility — upsells at checkout, post-purchase upsells, custom checkout flows. Furniture is high-AOV with strong related-item potential. See Branch Furniture's cart upsells in action → (add the Artisan Desk to cart to see the related-item upsell — Plus-only)
  • Shopify Audiences — marketing teams use Audiences for targeting and acquisition lift. Plus-only.
  • Shopify Flow — automation across returns, fraud, fulfillment, segmentation triggers, and marketing
  • Shopify Functions — custom discount logic, checkout customization, delivery rules
  • Dedicated success team — account-managed support through migration and beyond
  • Headroom for additional sub-brands — Advanced doesn't support multi-brand operations
Wichita Furniture is exactly the profile Plus is built for — an established, large multi-channel retailer with an ERP integration, a multi-brand roadmap, and a marketing-led operating model. The right question isn't "can we save on license cost?" — it's "what does the business need to support its operations and what's coming next, and which plan delivers that?" Plus is the plan that fits the business.

Appendix · The cost math

At $5M GMV, Plus costs ~$11.5K more than Advanced on the published rate card.

Plus carries a higher base license ($27,600/year) versus the Advanced plan ($3,588/year) — a $24,012 license premium. Plus's lower card processing rate (2.25% standard vs. Advanced's 2.5%) saves $12,500/year at $5M GMV on standard card transactions. Net: Plus costs ~$11,500/year more than Advanced at $5M online GMV. The crossover point where Plus becomes cheaper on pure license + processing is ~$9.6M.

The crossover point: Plus's $24K license premium and the 25bps card-rate savings cross at ~$9.6M online GMV ($24,012 ÷ 0.25% = $9.6M). Below $9.6M, Advanced is cheaper on a license + processing basis alone. Above $9.6M, Plus is cheaper — and Plus's advantage grows with every additional dollar of GMV.
How VPF affects this math: Plus charges the higher of $2,300/mo base OR 0.35% of monthly GMV (the "variable platform fee," or VPF). At $5M annual GMV, 0.35% × $5M = $17,500 — less than the $27,600 base. VPF does not apply at $5M. The base license is what you pay either way. VPF only kicks in above ~$7.9M annual GMV.

Assumptions: Plus online rates 2.25% + 30¢ (Standard Card) and 2.95% + 30¢ (Amex & Commercial); Advanced online rates 2.5% + 30¢ (Standard Card) and 3.1% + 30¢ (Premium). 30¢ per-transaction fee identical on both plans (cancels in comparison). Card processing math at $5M models standard card transactions only — a higher Amex / premium mix widens the differential (Plus is 15bps cheaper on Amex too) and moves the breakeven down. Excludes in-person processing (in-person rates also favor Plus). Plus rates can negotiate lower than 2.25% for higher-volume contracts — final rates depend on contract terms.

Appendix · Bridging the gap

How Plus features drive revenue that exceeds the cost premium.

The $11,500/year cost gap at $5M is modest in absolute terms — and small compared to the incremental revenue Plus's features generate when actively used. The conversation isn't about saving on license. It's about unlocking revenue and capability that the Advanced plan can't deliver. Here's how the gap is bridged.

1. Checkout extensibility — cart upsells ~$50K/yr at 1% AOV lift

Cart upsells (companion/recommended products surfaced at checkout) routinely lift average order value 3–10% in furniture and home retail. Furniture is a high-AOV category with strong related-item attach potential (chair → ottoman, sofa → side table, bed → mattress).

  • At $5M GMV, even a 1% AOV lift = ~$50,000/year incremental revenue
  • A 3% lift = ~$150,000/year; a 5% lift = ~$250,000/year
  • Plus-only — Advanced has no checkout extensibility
  • See Branch Furniture's cart upsells in action →

2. Shopify Flow — automation & cart abandonment recovery ~$100K/yr potential

Automated cart abandonment recovery workflows typically recover 5–12% of abandoned sessions. For a high-AOV considered-purchase category like furniture (where customers research and compare before buying), this is especially meaningful.

  • On $5M online GMV, even a 2–3% recovery uplift = ~$100,000+/year incremental revenue
  • Beyond abandonment: automated returns workflows, fraud routing, segmentation triggers, post-purchase nurture
  • Plus-only — Advanced requires manual ops or third-party automation tools (with their own subscription costs)

3. Shopify Audiences — improved marketing ROAS ~$150K/yr at 15% CPA lift

Audiences uses Shopify-wide buyer signal data to improve ad targeting on Meta and Google. Plus customers report 10–25% CPA improvements, especially for high-AOV brands where the value of better targeting is amplified.

  • On $1M ad spend (typical for a $5M DTC operation), even a 15% CPA improvement = ~$150,000/year freed for additional acquisition (or equivalent revenue from same spend)
  • Compounds with The Gallery launch — better targeting from day one on the luxury sub-brand
  • Plus-only

4. Shopify Functions — custom commerce logic at checkout

Functions enables custom delivery options, conditional financing, and custom discount logic at the most conversion-sensitive moment in the funnel.

  • Custom delivery options at checkout: white-glove delivery, scheduled delivery, room-of-choice — meaningful conversion drivers for furniture at high AOV
  • Conditional financing offers surfaced at checkout (relevant given Wichita's existing financing partnerships)
  • Custom discount logic: bundle pricing, loyalty multipliers, threshold-triggered promotions
  • Plus-only — Advanced has standard checkout only

5. Higher API rate limits — STORIS reliability & revenue protection

Reliable inventory sync between STORIS and Shopify directly protects revenue. Overselling incidents (selling items that aren't actually in stock) lead to cancelled orders, customer service costs, and brand trust erosion — outsized impact in high-AOV furniture where each lost sale is meaningful.

  • Plus's higher API throughput supports near-real-time inventory sync with STORIS
  • Advanced's lower API limits can cause sync delays, increasing overselling risk during traffic spikes (holiday, promotions)
  • Revenue protection isn't always visible on the P&L — but the avoided cost of cancelled orders and damaged trust is real

6. Dedicated success team — strategic guidance

Plus customers get strategic counsel and access to Shopify's expertise network — particularly valuable during a complex multi-brand migration like Wichita Furniture + The Gallery.

  • Strategic guidance on roadmap opportunities (Shop Pay, B2B channel, headless commerce if relevant)
  • Direct access to Shopify product and engineering teams for complex implementation questions
  • Helps avoid costly platform missteps in a phase where they'd be most expensive to undo
The bottom line: the $11,500/year cost gap at $5M GMV is typically eclipsed within the first 1–2 months of running on Plus when these features are actively used. Even modest performance against industry benchmarks on any one of cart upsells, abandonment recovery, or Audiences ROAS generates incremental revenue that's a multiple of the entire annual cost premium. The conversation isn't about saving on license cost — it's about unlocking revenue and capability that Advanced can't deliver.
