Total cost of ownership

Cost-savings is a TCO question — not just license.

The fair comparison is the all-in cost of Magento today vs. all-in Shopify Plus — across license, payments, implementation, integrations, apps, and ongoing dev. Most of the savings come from native Shopify replacing standalone apps + reduced ongoing dev dependence, not the license itself.

Current state — Magento

The "from" side of the TCO comparison
  • Magento Commerce license + hosting — annual platform cost
  • Magento dev team / agency — ongoing engineering investment to keep the site shipping
  • Maintenance, security, performance work — patches, upgrades, performance tooling (e.g., Yottaa)
  • App stack — itemized monthly spend across Searchspring, Customily, Klaviyo, Attentive, Yotpo, Narvar, Avalara, Forter, Ketch, Klarna, BlogMX, Mandrill, etc.
  • Payment processing — current annual processing cost + chargeback / fraud losses
  • International / multicurrency — current cross-border setup cost

Future state — Shopify Plus

The "to" side of the TCO comparison
  • Plus license — $2,300/mo minimum (unlocked with 3-year commit) or 0.35% VPF, whichever is higher
  • Shopify Payments — 2.25% + 30¢ standard / 2.95% + 30¢ Amex & Commercial
  • Lazer implementation — one-time $329,500 ($40K Discovery + $289,500 build); optional $48K extended hypercare; optional $16K/mo retainer
  • Consolidated app stack — Yottaa, BlogMX, and Mandrill retired (replaced by Shopify native capabilities); continued vendors (Klaviyo, Attentive, Yotpo, Narvar, Klarna, Forter, Ketch, Avalara) carry forward
  • New vendors — Clutch (gift cards), Feedonomics (feeds), Matrixify (migration), Swym (wishlists), Global-e (international/MOR)
  • Dedicated Shopify account team — included; reduces ongoing agency dependence vs. Magento

Where TCO savings typically come from on a Magento → Plus migration

  • Native Shopify capabilities replacing standalone apps: Yottaa (site speed) → Shopify theme performance; BlogMX → Shopify native blog; Mandrill → Klaviyo/NetSuite/Narvar transactional email routing. Removed monthly app spend stacks up over a 3-year horizon.
  • Reduced ongoing dev / agency reliance: Cart Transform Function, Shopify Flow, and the Shopify admin let the e-commerce team ship without engineering on most workflows. Customers commonly reduce agency retainer spend post-launch as the team takes more ownership.

Pricing — Shopify Plus + merchant services

What Plus costs, and how merchant services rates work.

Shopify Plus is our enterprise plan for established retailers operating at scale. License pricing is structured as the higher of a base fee or a variable platform fee (VPF) based on GMV. Card processing rates are tiered by card type.

Shopify Plus license

Standard 1-year US Plus contract
  • Base license: $2,300/mo ($27,600/year) — the floor
  • Variable Platform Fee (VPF): 0.35% of monthly GMV — applies when 0.35% × GMV exceeds the base
  • You pay the higher of the two, not both. Below ~$7.9M annual GMV (~$657K/month), you pay just the base. Above, VPF replaces the base and scales with revenue.

Shopify Payments — merchant services rates

Online card-not-present, Shopify Payments
Standard Card 2.25% + $0.30 per transaction
Amex & Commercial 2.95% + $0.30 per transaction

Shopify Payments is the named gateway in the Lazer SOW — fully integrated with Plus, no separate processor relationship to manage post-launch.

Open input we'll need together: annual GMV is the single biggest input to finalizing both the Plus license math (whether VPF applies) and the payment-processing commit. AOV is in hand at $312 — order volume is the gap. Once we have annualized GMV, we can lock the final pricing model.
Variable Platform Fee (VPF)

Plus license scales with revenue — here's how it works.

Shopify Plus charges the higher of two amounts: a flat base license ($2,300/month) or a percentage of monthly GMV (0.35%). At low GMV, the base applies and Plus is a fixed cost. As revenue grows, VPF takes over and license scales with the business — but it's never both at once.

How it plays out at different GMV bands

Below ~$7.9M annual GMV
You pay $2,300/mo base. VPF (0.35% × monthly GMV) is less than $2,300 — base license applies. License is a flat cost regardless of monthly fluctuations.
~$7.9M – ~$10M annual GMV
VPF starts to exceed base. You pay 0.35% of monthly GMV. At $10M annual GMV, license is ~$35,000/year (vs. $27,600 base). Plus continues to deliver value (lower card rates, full feature set, dedicated account team) that compounds with revenue.
$10M+ annual GMV
VPF scales linearly with revenue. At $20M GMV, license is ~$70,000/year; at $50M, ~$175,000/year. Even at high GMV, the per-dollar cost is 0.35% of license + 2.25% of processing — a meaningful improvement over Magento + standalone processing for any merchant Catbird's size.

Tech requirements

The architecture, in one read.

Catbird's post-replatform stack keeps NetSuite as the system of record and SuperSync as the only middleware. Shopify becomes the storefront, payments engine, and configurator host. Several decisions remain open and will be locked in during Discovery.

Confirmed architecture

From the Lazer SOW
  • Storefront: Shopify Plus, mobile-first Shopify 2.0 Liquid theme (not headless)
  • ERP: NetSuite — system of record for inventory, pricing, orders, fulfillment decisions
  • Middleware / IPaaS: SuperSync — bridges Shopify ↔ NetSuite, only middleware in the integration path
  • WMS: Deposco — accessed through NetSuite (not direct)
  • POS: NewStore — staying off Shopify for initial launch (future project)
  • Payments: Shopify Payments (named in SOW)
  • International: Global-e (multicurrency + Merchant of Record) — recommended vendor
  • Tax: Avalara via Shopify extension at checkout
  • Configurators: Confetti + Memento built on metaobjects + metafields + Cart Transform Functions; engraving with emoji subset; add-on chains/charms via metaobject configuration
  • BOPIS: Williamsburg + Soho locations at checkout (orders identified for in-store fulfillment via NetSuite routing)

Open decisions (locked during Discovery)

Tech decisions still to be made

  • Visualizer: Customily evaluation — build vs. buy decision
