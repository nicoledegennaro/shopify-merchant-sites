Overview

Where Shopify fits

Lighting Unlimited is a lighting & electrical distributor serving the greater Phoenix Valley from four branches, with roughly 92% of sales B2B. The opportunity isn't a new sales team — it's giving the customers you already serve a faster way to buy.

The problem today

Nearly every order runs through a person — walk-ins, phone calls, and manually written tickets on a pre-coded pricing matrix. It works, but as the company scales, the overhead to handle all those manual transactions gets heavy. There's no online interface for the customers who just want to log in and re-order at their agreed pricing.

The goal

A branded, gated storefront (e.g. an LU-owned domain "powered by Shopify") where account customers log in to their own tier pricing and self-serve — while DDI stays your source of truth and the big, complex spec jobs stay exactly where they are.

Guardrail we'll respect: manufacturers (Acuity, Philips, Lutron, Minka, etc.) restrict public online listing. Everything sits behind a login on your own branded site — not on a public marketplace — which keeps you inside your buying-group and channel agreements.
Current State

How it works right now

A quick map of today's stack and the three ways customers buy — so we only rebuild what's worth rebuilding.

Systems today

  • ERP: DDI (distributor ERP) — stays the system of record; you may evaluate NetSuite down the line
  • E-commerce: none currently (previous site retired); manual listings on Amazon & eBay, low volume
  • Payments: a separate card processor (buying-group negotiated rates); most sales are on terms / AR
  • Buying group: Affiliated Distributors — channel & pricing agreements to preserve

Three ways customers buy

  • Counter & pop-up pricing — walk-in / phone, pre-coded tier pricing. ← the best fit to move online
  • Outside sales / spec jobs — complex quotes, large POs (e.g. $100k retrofits). Stays in DDI
  • Municipal / contract — blanket POs & pre-negotiated annual pricing; one city requires an online PO portal

Pricing complexity

Tiers today are bronze / silver / gold, moving toward a cleaner bronze / silver / gold / platinum model — with customer-category modifiers (electrician vs. general contractor vs. property management). Net terms (net 30 / 45 / 60) are common. This tier count is the main thing that decides the plan (below).

Why the revenue uplift is real (not wishful)

  • Reorder convenience. Accounts that can log in and re-order at their own pricing buy more often — no waiting on a callback or a counter visit.
  • Bigger baskets. A browsable, always-on catalog surfaces items a phone order skips; B2B buyers self-serving typically add lines they'd otherwise forget.
  • Capacity to grow without headcount. Every order that self-serves frees your counter/phone team to chase new accounts and quotes instead of keying tickets.
  • Always open. Customers order after hours and on weekends — demand you can't capture on the phone today.

What your owner & CFO will likely ask — and the answer

  • "It works today — why spend?" Your customers are asking to self-serve, you just won a city contract that requires an online portal, and this scales counter/phone volume without adding headcount.
  • "Is the DDI integration risky?" Solo Media is a Shopify Platinum partner (500+ migrations, ERP integrations included). Worst case, we use the same CSV / scheduled-sync model you already run with SmartSheets — and DDI stays your source of truth.
  • "Do we have to rip out our POS / payment processing?" No. Online-first; your DDI POS keeps running in parallel. In-store/POS is a later phase.
  • "Who maintains it — we have no IT?" Shopify runs the platform (uptime, security, upgrades, servers). You edit content yourselves and Solo Media hands over documentation (optional retainer for ongoing custom work). Plus you get Shopify Plus support, a Customer Success Manager, and Nicole + Angel supporting you long-term.
  • "We just moved to a new payment processor a year ago — not doing that again." You don't have to. This is a new online channel; your counter/in-store setup and the processor you just switched to keep running untouched. For the online store you can turn on Shopify Payments or keep your current processor as an approved third-party gateway on Plus (+~0.2%). Nothing here undoes last year's switch.
  • "We're spending elsewhere right now — hiring and a buyout." This is sized to protect that spend, not compete with it. The build is a fixed one-time $30–35K on milestones (not upfront), and self-serve grows counter/account volume without adding headcount — so it stretches the hiring budget. With the Sept 30 incentive and the post-dated agreement, your first platform payment isn't due until ~Nov 30, easing cash flow while the buyout closes.
  • "What if we move off DDI to a new ERP around 2030?" Shopify sits on top of your ERP through a defined sync — it isn't welded to DDI. If you move to NetSuite (already on your radar) or another ERP later, you re-point the same integration to the new system; the storefront, customer accounts, tier pricing and order history all stay put. You swap what feeds the store, not the store itself — and connectors exist for NetSuite and the other major ERPs.
  • "Timeline?" Jan 1 is feasible — an OK from the owners this week, a Sept 30 signature/kickoff, and DDI as the critical path, so the sooner we start the safer the date.
