What you need to do

Clear the supportability review — a quick claims cleanup.

Like every card network and processor, Shopify Payments requires product and marketing claims to be compliant — nothing that says a product diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents disease. This is set out in Shopify's Acceptable Use Policy — the same policy for every merchant. For a health & science brand it's a standard, one-time review, and it's almost entirely wording, not product.

  • Scan product pages & marketing for disease/drug-style language — "cures," "treats," "prevents [condition]," "clinically proven to…," or claims implying a medical outcome.
  • Convert to compliant structure/function claims — "supports," "helps maintain," "promotes" — with the standard disclaimer ("These statements have not been evaluated by the FDA…").
  • Remove or substantiate any specific therapeutic/medical claims you can't back with allowed evidence.
  • Reply to the supportability team confirming the updates — that clears the review and unblocks activation.

We'll flag the specific items so you're not guessing, and can point you to how peer supplement/science brands word theirs. Most edits are copy changes on a handful of pages — not reformulation, and not a change to what you sell.

On the paperwork

Don't let the legal language give you pause.

It's the same framework every merchant signs.

The Shopify Payments terms, the standard merchant agreement, and the claims/compliance language you'll see — including Shopify's Acceptable Use Policy — aren't specific to Prodrome and aren't a signal of concern about your business. It's the identical framework that applies to every merchant on the platform — from a one-person shop to the largest enterprises — and it exists to keep the platform compliant and to protect you.

Nothing in it is unusual for your category, and it doesn't change how you operate. You can review the full framework any time in Shopify's Legal center (Terms of Service, Payments Terms, and the AUP). If any clause raises a question, we'll walk you (or your counsel) through it — but it's routine, universal, and nothing to get hung up on.

Activation

What to bring, and what changing payments looks like.

You're already on Plus, so switching your payment provider is a settings change — and you can set up Shopify Payments without turning Authorize.net off first, so there's no interruption to checkout. You flip to Shopify Payments as your sole provider only once you're approved and ready.

What to have ready (U.S.)

  • The store owner to activate — only the owner can switch to Shopify Payments (staff can't).
  • Two-step authentication enabled on the account.
  • Business type + legal registered nameMatching your IRS documents exactly (e.g., SS-4 / CP575 / 147c).
  • EINOr SSN/ITIN if you file as an individual.
  • Authorized representative's detailsName, DOB, SSN/ITIN, home address, title — plus a photo ID if asked.
  • Anyone who owns 25%+ of the business — same details.
  • Business bank account — routing + account numbers (for payouts).
  • Statement descriptor + a phone number to show on customers' card statements.

The process — switching from Authorize.net

  1. Settings → Payments → Activate Shopify Payments (store owner).
  2. Enter your business + representative details and statement descriptor.
  3. Connect your payout bank account and set your payout schedule (daily / weekly / monthly).
  4. Submit for verification — usually reviewed within ~24 hours (status shows "pending").
  5. Authorize.net keeps running through this — zero checkout downtime while you're approved.
  6. Once approved, make Shopify Payments your primary gateway — new orders, new subscriptions and any renewed card bill on it. Shop Pay turns on and the 0.2% fee is waived on that volume.
  7. Keep Authorize.net connected as the secondary (legacy) gateway for existing subscriptions so they bill uninterrupted — they move to Shopify Payments one-by-one as cards renew ("slow-drip"). Don't fully deactivate it while subs still bill there.
  8. Place a test order, confirm your first payout — you're live.

Requirements shown are for U.S. businesses; the exact list is confirmed in your admin. Only the store owner can activate or switch. References: Setting up Shopify Payments · U.S. requirements · Switching from a third-party provider.
